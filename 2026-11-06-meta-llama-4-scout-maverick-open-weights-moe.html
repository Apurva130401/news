Meta FAIR Releases Llama 4 Scout and Maverick: Open-Weight Frontier Mixture-of-Experts with Native Multimodality and 1M Token Context

By SyncFlo AI Editorial Team · November 6, 2026 · 11 min read

MENLO PARK, Calif. — November 6, 2026 — In a watershed moment for the global open-source AI ecosystem, Meta AI and its Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) division today officially released the weights, training code, and inference recipes for the Llama 4 model family, headlined by Llama 4 Scout and Llama 4 Maverick.

Marking the first time a frontier-tier lab has open-sourced natively multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures with production-grade 1-million-token context windows, Llama 4 decisively closes the performance gap between proprietary closed-source models and community-accessible foundation intelligence. The release provides enterprises and developers with complete data sovereignty, unrestricted fine-tuning, and on-premises deployment capabilities.

1. Scout and Maverick: Two Precision-Engineered MoE Configurations

Rather than shipping a single rigid model size, Meta engineered two complementary architectures designed to address the full spectrum from cost-efficient enterprise edge deployments to extreme frontier reasoning:

Llama 4 Family Architecture Comparison

Llama 4 Scout (109B / 17B Active)
  • Total Parameters: 109 Billion
  • Active Per Token: 17 Billion (16 Experts, top-2 routing)
  • Context Length: 1,000,000 tokens native (YaRN-scaled RoPE)
  • Target Use: High-throughput agent swarms, long-document retrieval, edge server clusters, and sub-20ms interactive inference.
Llama 4 Maverick (400B MoE)
  • Total Parameters: 400 Billion
  • Active Per Token: 64 Billion (32 Experts, top-2 routing)
  • Context Length: 256,000 tokens
  • Target Use: Frontier scientific discovery, autonomous competitive coding, formal mathematical verification, and cross-modal video reasoning.

2. Native Multimodality: Abandoning Fragmented Encoders

A critical architectural innovation in Llama 4 is the transition to Unified Multimodal Tokenization. Previous open-source models pasted pre-trained vision encoders (like CLIP or SigLIP) and audio models (like Whisper) onto frozen language backbones via cross-attention adapter layers. This fragmented approach suffered from information loss at modality boundaries and high latency overhead.

In contrast, Llama 4 was pre-trained from token zero on an interleaved dataset of text, code, high-resolution imagery, video sequences, and raw 48kHz audio spectrograms. The shared transformer backbone processes visual tokens and phoneme tokens natively within the same attention layers, granting the model unprecedented intuition for physical scenes, spoken emotional inflection, and multi-image spatial reasoning.

"Real intelligence does not separate seeing from thinking or hearing from speaking. By training Llama 4 natively across modalities within a sparse mixture-of-experts structure, the experts specialize organically—some focusing on geometric invariant representations, others on mathematical syntax, and others on auditory cadence."
— Yann LeCun, Chief AI Scientist at Meta

3. Speculative Tool Execution: Accelerating Agent Swarms

For autonomous multi-agent systems, latency during tool invocation has long been a bottleneck. Every time an agent decides to call a database query or search API, inference pauses while the tool runs, and the subsequent generation must re-encode context.

Llama 4 introduces Speculative Agentic Tool Calling. While the main expert path generates the reasoning chain, auxiliary lightweight drafting heads predict candidate API arguments in parallel. If the prediction matches the validator schema, the tool executes concurrently with next-token decoding, slashing agentic loop response times by up to 3.5x on complex workflows.

5. Day-One Ecosystem Integration and Open Weights Availability

Effective immediately, the complete model weights, FP8/INT4 quantization formats, and fine-tuning check-points are available under the permissive Meta Open License via:

  • Hugging Face Hub: Model cards, GGUF/EXL2 quantized checkpoints, and multi-GPU distributed weights.
  • Ollama & vLLM: One-line local terminal execution (ollama run llama4-scout) with tensor-parallel support.
  • Hardware Acceleration Partners: Day-one driver optimizations across NVIDIA TensorRT-LLM, AMD ROCm 6.3, Apple Metal Silicon, and AWS Inferentia3.
