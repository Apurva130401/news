NVIDIA Keynote at SC26 Debuts Vera Rubin NVL576 Supercluster & Apollo-2 Industrial Physics Foundation Models: Ushering in the Exascale AI Factory Era

By SyncFlo AI Editorial Team · November 12, 2026 · 14 min read

ATLANTA, Georgia — November 12, 2026 — Opening the annual Supercomputing 2026 (SC26) conference before thousands of high-performance computing (HPC) researchers, national laboratory directors, and enterprise architects, NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang delivered a landmark keynote address announcing the commercial production of the Vera Rubin NVL576 liquid-cooled supercluster and the global release of Apollo-2, a groundbreaking suite of open-weights, physics-grounded foundation models engineered to revolutionize industrial simulation, materials discovery, and computational fluid dynamics.

The dual announcements represent what Huang termed the "irreversible confluence of scientific high-performance computing and generative physical AI." As traditional supercomputing clusters grapple with thermal thresholds and quadratic scaling walls, the Vera Rubin NVL576 redefines the unit of compute from individual accelerators to a monolithic, 576-GPU optically networked computing organism capable of delivering an unprecedented 28 Exaflops of FP4 AI inference and 1.4 Exaflops of IEEE double-precision FP64 scientific simulation within an ultra-dense, direct-to-chip liquid-cooled footprint.

1. The Architectural Marvel: Vera Rubin NVL576 and NVLink-6 Photonics

Named in honor of pioneering astronomer Vera Rubin, the Rubin architecture moves beyond standard electronic inter-rack interconnects by deploying sixth-generation NVLink-6 Co-Packaged Optics (CPO). Operating at 3.6 Terabits per second per lane, NVLink-6 allows all 576 GPUs across eight unified compute cabinets to act as a single, coherent memory domain boasting 144 Terabytes of ultra-high-bandwidth HBM4 memory with an aggregate bisection bandwidth exceeding 1.1 Petabytes per second.

  • 576 Rubin GPU Monolith: Features third-generation Transformer and Diffusion Engines with native FP4, FP6, and microscopic 3-bit block floating point precision formats tailored for frontier reasoning architectures.
  • Vera CPU Subsystem: Powered by custom 288-core ARM Neoverse V3 server CPUs, delivering a 3.4x throughput uplift in scientific preprocessing, graph traversal, and sparse memory indexing over previous Grace CPU architectures.
  • Direct-to-Chip Dual-Phase Liquid Cooling: Engineered in collaboration with CoolIT Systems and Vertiv, the system extracts 100% of heat via closed-loop warm-water manifolds, operating at an astonishing Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of 1.05 and recovering 90% of waste heat for municipal heating grids.
  • Quantum-X1600 InfiniBand: Offers 1,600 Gbps per port throughput with in-network computing hardware engines (SHARP v4), allowing distributed physical AI models to train across thousands of nodes with near-linear 98.2% scaling efficiency.

2. Apollo-2: Physics Foundation Models for Industrial Digital Twins

While the Rubin NVL576 represents a quantum leap in silicon density, NVIDIA's release of Apollo-2 is poised to fundamentally disrupt computational engineering. Unlike generic large language models that merely mimic text patterns, Apollo-2 is a neural operator architecture trained directly on first-principles physics, differential equations, and petabyte-scale simulation databases generated over decades by global supercomputing institutions.

Apollo-2 comprises three specialized domain foundation backbones:

  • Apollo-2 AeroFluids: Employs Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) and mesh graph neural nets to solve 3D turbulent Navier-Stokes equations across hypersonic aircraft airframes, formula-one vehicle dynamics, and atmospheric micro-climates up to 1,200 times faster than CPU-based numerical solvers.
  • Apollo-2 Materials & Quantum: Integrates equivariant spherical harmonics to predict molecular orbital interactions, enabling battery scientists to simulate lithium-dendrite degradation across millions of candidate polymer electrolytes in real time.
  • Apollo-2 Continuum Mechanics: Computes finite element stress, thermo-mechanical fatigue, and nonlinear plastic deformation across intricate manufacturing components, directly ingesting CAD geometries from Siemens NX, Dassault CATIA, and Autodesk Inventor.

3. Global Supercomputing Deployments: Oak Ridge, TACC Horizon & RIKEN

NVIDIA revealed that Vera Rubin NVL576 systems have already been contracted for flagship installations at the world's most prestigious scientific supercomputing facilities. The Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) will integrate 64 Rubin NVL576 racks into its upcoming Horizon supercomputing facility, creating America's largest academic AI discovery engine.

Simultaneously, the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) in Tennessee and Japan's RIKEN Center for Computational Science confirmed orders for multi-exascale Rubin clusters to accelerate carbon capture catalysts, plasma confinement modeling in ITER tokamak fusion reactors, and next-generation planetary climate modeling.

4. Enterprise Impact and Sovereign Industrial AI

In addition to public scientific agencies, Fortune 500 industrial giants are lining up to adopt Apollo-2 and Rubin infrastructure within sovereign data centers. Aerospace leaders such as Airbus and Boeing, semiconductor fabricators including TSMC and ASML, and energy conglomerates like Shell are leveraging NVIDIA Omniverse Enterprise coupled with Apollo-2 to operate live, self-optimizing digital twins of entire factories, refineries, and fabrication plants.

By embedding neural physics directly into factory floor telemetry, operators can predict component failure weeks before structural fatigue manifests, optimizing maintenance schedules and eliminating billions of dollars in unplanned downtime.
