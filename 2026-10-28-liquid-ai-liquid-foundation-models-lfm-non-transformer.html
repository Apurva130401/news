Liquid AI Unveils Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs): MIT Spinout Crushes Transformer Memory Bottlenecks with Dynamic Differential Equations

By SyncFlo AI Editorial Team · October 28, 2026 · 9 min read

BOSTON, MA — October 28, 2026 — In an architectural breakthrough that challenges the near-decade dominance of the transformer architecture, MIT spinout Liquid AI has officially unveiled its family of Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs).

Founded by prominent MIT roboticists and computer scientists—including Ramin Hasani, Alexander Amini, Mathias Lechner, and MIT CSAIL Director Daniela Rus—Liquid AI has built the world's first general-purpose generative AI models powered entirely by continuous-time dynamical systems and neural differential equations rather than discrete self-attention mechanisms.

1. Breaking Free from the Transformer Attention Bottleneck

Since the seminal 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need," generative AI has rested almost exclusively on the Transformer architecture. While transformers excel at capturing correlations across token sequences, they suffer from fundamental computational bottlenecks:

  • Quadratic Scaling Complexity: Standard transformer self-attention scales quadratically, $O(N^2)$, with sequence length, making ultra-long context reasoning computationally ruinous.
  • KV-Cache Memory Bloat: As generation continues, storing the Key-Value (KV) cache for millions of tokens consumes tens of gigabytes of GPU VRAM per user, limiting deployment on local edge hardware, mobile devices, and robots.

Liquid Foundation Models resolve this through Liquid Neural Networks (LNNs). Instead of recalculating pairwise attention across every historical token, LFMs model information as smooth, continuous-time trajectories governed by non-linear differential equations. During inference, LFMs maintain a constant memory footprint regardless of how long the input sequence stretches.

"Nature doesn't process reality in static, discrete token blocks. Physical processes, human conversation, sensory streams, and thoughts unfold continuously. Liquid Foundation Models bridge classical dynamical systems with modern deep learning, allowing models to adapt dynamically to incoming data while requiring a fraction of the compute and memory needed by transformers."
— Ramin Hasani, CEO & Co-Founder, Liquid AI

Liquid Foundation Model (LFM) Family Specifications

LFM-1B Edge Champion Operates with under 1 GB of memory footprint, running at over 100 tokens/sec on Raspberry Pi, Apple Silicon, Qualcomm Snapdragon, and automotive chips.
LFM-3B Foundation Tier Outperforms competing 3B and 7B transformer models (including Llama-3.2 3B and Phi-3.5) across reasoning, language, and coding benchmarks.
LFM-40B MoE Enterprise A mixture-of-experts dynamical architecture activating 12B parameters per token, delivering frontier reasoning with zero KV-cache degradation.

2. Native Multimodal Continuous Reasoning

Because LFMs are inherently formulated over continuous time, they possess a native advantage over multimodal time-series streams. While transformers require slicing audio, video, sensor readings, and robotic telemetry into artificial token slices, LFMs digest analog inputs as seamless continuous waveforms.

In robotics and autonomous vehicles, this eliminates the perception latency inherent in video frame sampling. An LFM-powered controller can adjust steering angles, robotic grip pressures, and spatial flight trajectories at thousands of updates per second with zero discrete frame jitter.

3. Benchmarks: Crushing Compute and Memory Limits

On standard LLM evaluation benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, and ARC-Challenge), Liquid AI's models demonstrate dramatic parameter efficiency:

  • 32K to 1M Token Contexts: Where transformer inference speeds degrade severely as the KV-cache expands to fill memory, LFM token generation throughput remains flat and constant from token 1 to token 1,000,000.
  • Energy Consumption: On server-grade H100 and B200 clusters, serving LFM models requires up to 65% less electrical power compared to equivalent-sized transformer architectures, unlocking substantial cost reductions for enterprise scale.

4. Strategic Hardware Partnerships: Apple, Qualcomm, and Nvidia

Liquid AI has engineered its kernels to execute natively across diverse hardware targets without requiring exotic silicon. Through optimized compilation layers, LFMs run at near peak memory bandwidth on Nvidia CUDA GPUs, Apple Neural Engine (ANE), Qualcomm Hexagon NPUs, AMD ROCm accelerators, and standard Intel/ARM CPUs.

This universal hardware compatibility enables consumer electronics manufacturers, automotive OEMs, and defense contractors to embed fully offline conversational intelligence into smart devices, drones, and connected machinery without cloud connectivity.
